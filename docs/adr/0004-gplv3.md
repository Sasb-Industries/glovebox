# 0004 — License: GPLv3

**Status:** Accepted (2026-10-01)

## Context
Goal: free for anyone to use; nobody should be able to fork it, change a little, and sell a closed copy.

Options considered: MIT (permits closed commercial forks), GPLv3, PolyForm Noncommercial (bans commercial use but isn't OSI open source and technically excludes for-profit workplace use), PolyForm Shield (bans even free competing forks).

## Decision
GPLv3, public repository from day one.

Any distributed fork must also be GPLv3 with full source available, so it can always be rebuilt for free. That makes "fork, tweak, sell closed" illegal and pointless.

## Consequences
- No license can physically prevent secret violation. In practice:
  - Electron apps ship their JavaScript, so a "closed" fork is inspectable.
  - Selling a Drive client publicly requires Google's restricted-scope verification under a real identity.
  - Violations can be met with DMCA takedowns or a cease-and-desist.
- The copyright holder can relicense future versions; already-released versions keep GPLv3. Once outside contributions land, relicensing needs contributors' agreement.
